About me

Robotics and Control Engineer, in the final year of an M.Sc. at McGill.

I like control problems where the model is never quite right: an autonomous wheelchair with no access to precise localization such as GNSS, a quadruped picking up a payload, a cobot sharing a load with a person whose hand trembles. My work is building models good enough to control with, and controllers robust enough to cope with what the models miss.

At McGill's NeuRoc Lab I tune a robot arm under Cartesian admittance–impedance control while it is physically coupled to a MyoSuite musculoskeletal arm model in MuJoCo, and I use reinforcement learning to train that musculoskeletal model to move and perform a task. Across the controller gains, I map the trade-off between load support, intent following and tremor attenuation as the basis for an assistive co-manipulation strategy. I am now applying the controller to a real Franka Research 3 (FR3) arm. I am also a teaching assistant for four mechanical engineering courses, including Control Systems and Applied Electronics & Instrumentation.

Alongside my M.Sc., I worked as a Robotics Engineer at a robotics startup on the ROS 2/Nav2 navigation, EKF sensor fusion and MPPI controller tuning of a smart wheelchair. For my B.Sc. thesis I derived the full-body dynamic model of a quadruped and ran real-time MPC in MuJoCo, with reinforcement learning correcting what the model misses. Before that, as an R&D robotics engineer, I designed the entire electrical system of a blood flow restriction medical device and helped build it by hand. I'm looking for robotics, control and mechatronics work where models meet hardware.
